      What it leaves out

      • New builds (HST, Tarion, development charges) and condo status certificates.
      • Homes outside Ontario, and multi-unit or non-resident purchases.
      • Lender fees, appraisal fees and your first mortgage payment.
      • Partial rebates when only one buyer is a first-time buyer.
      • The land transfer tax refunds need you to have never owned a home. The 30-year insured amortization uses a looser test (no home you owned and lived in during the last four years), so some repeat buyers qualify for it too. The toggle uses the stricter one.

      This is a planning tool, not advice. Your lawyer's statement of adjustments is the final word.
